Defining the "Who"

Case Study: Airbnb
Airbnb's MVS wasn't "everyone needing a room." It was specifically designers attending the IDSA conference in SF who couldn't find hotel space.

Focus on the smallest segment with shared needs that you can sell to repeatedly.

The Four U's of Problem Evaluation

Unworkable
Case Study: Amazon (Books)
Discovery was unworkable. Physical bookstores couldn't stock 1M+ titles, making it inefficient to find niche books.

The current situation is broken or inefficient.

Unavoidable
Case Study: HealthVerity
Compliance with HIPAA/GDPR is unavoidable for pharma. They must solve the consent management problem to use data.

The customer has to solve this (mandates/laws).

Urgent

The problem requires immediate attention.

Underserved

Existing solutions don't adequately address the need.

The Three D's

1. Disruptive
Definition: Makes an existing market more accessible, cheaper, or efficient.

Example: Cloud Computing (AWS) made server infrastructure accessible to anyone, disrupting the data center market.

How does this product change the market economics?

2. Discontinuous
Definition: Enables things that were previously impossible.

Case Study: WeavrCare
Patient-owned, immutable health records were impossible with paper or centralized servers. Blockchain makes the "sovereign record" possible.

What can users do now that they absolutely could not do before?
